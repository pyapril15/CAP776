## Relationship Analysis

1. **Coding ↔ Energy** — r = **+0.21** (weak positive)
   Days with more coding time tended to coincide, mildly, with slightly higher reported energy — but the relationship is too weak to call coding a driver of energy; other daily factors likely matter more.

2. **Sleep ↔ Energy** — r = **−0.26** (weak negative)
   Counterintuitively, more sleep showed a slight negative association with energy. This is a weak effect and shouldn't be overstated, but it's consistent with the idea that oversleeping (rather than sleep itself) can leave someone feeling less energized than a well-timed, moderate amount would. The raw data doesn't distinguish "amount of sleep" from "sleep quality" or "wake time," which is a limitation worth naming.

3. **Study ↔ Satisfaction** — r = **+0.06** (negligible)
   Essentially no linear relationship. Satisfaction on a given day was apparently driven by something other than how much was studied — an honest null result, not a finding to force into a narrative.
